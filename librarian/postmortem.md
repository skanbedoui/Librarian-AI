# Postmortem: what went wrong and what we learned

Old result files are kept in `eval/results/history/` so every number below can be checked.

## 1. The query rewrite made answers worse (88.7% → 99.2% after the fix)

**What happened.** The first full run of the baseline scored **88.7%**. Several answerable
questions got "I don't know", e.g. *"What GPA do I need to make the Dean's List?"*. Retrieval
on its own found the Dean's List page at rank 1, so search wasn't the problem. The full pipeline
did lose it, though.

**Root cause.** The rewrite step turned the question into *"What is the required Grade Point
Average to qualify for the Dean's List at the Mediterranean Institute of Technology?"*. The words
"Mediterranean Institute of Technology" and "Grade Point Average" appear on dozens of pages, so
they pushed the history page and the GPA-calculation page above the Dean's List page.

**Fix.** We now search with *original question + rewrite* together. The rewrite can add words,
but it can no longer remove the student's own words. We also told the rewriter not to add the
university's name.

**Lesson.** Every "smart" step in a pipeline can hurt. The retrieval eval used the raw
question, so it never saw this. **Evaluate the full pipeline, not just its parts.**

## 2. "I don't know", but then answering anyway

**What happened.** (a) The bot sometimes wrote *"The guides do not specify the gym hours. However…"*
Our detector looked for one exact sentence, so this didn't count as "I don't know". (b) After we
allowed one sentence of related info after "I don't know", the small model (`gpt-4o-mini`) began
starting correct answers with *"I don't know based on the student guides. However, …"*. That
dropped the optimised version to **94.3%** (`history/generation_optimised_v1.json`), which broke the
"lose at most 2 points" budget.

**Fix.** The rule now reads: say the sentence *alone* when nothing answers the question, and never
start with it otherwise. Both versions went back to ~99%.

**Lesson.** Smaller models follow prompts that contain exceptions less reliably. When you
change the model, re-check the prompt.

## 3. Our gold answers had mistakes

The evaluation set was generated by an LLM, and our review found three bad items:

- *"When are the Spring Term Final Exams?"*: the gold said **May 4–11**. The guide says
  **May 5–11**. The calendar is a table that `pypdf` flattens into
  `Spring Reading Days May 3rd May 4th … Final Exams May 5th May 11th`, and the question generator
  misread it. Our bot was right and the judge marked it wrong.
- *"What are the university opening hours Monday–Friday?"*: the gold took the **cafeteria** hours
  (8:30–2:30) from the passage. We removed it.
- *"What time does the cafeteria close on Fridays?"* was on our hand-written **unanswerable**
  list, but the guide does give the cafeteria hours. The bot answered correctly and was marked
  wrong. We removed it.

**Lesson.** An eval set is code too, and it has bugs. Read the failures one by one before
trusting a score, because sometimes the bot is right and the test is wrong.

## 4. Our evaluation is too easy, so the scores look better than reality

- The questions were generated **from the chunks themselves**, so they reuse the passage's exact
  words. That's why keyword search (BM25) beats vector search at Recall@1 in our ablation, and
  why Recall@5 saturates near 1.00. Real students write *"can I redo a class I flunked?"*, not
  *"Can I retake a course if I received a failing grade?"*.
- Faithfulness and citation accuracy came out at **100%**. That's suspicious: the judge
  (`gpt-4.1-mini`) is in the same model family as the generator and may be lenient. The human
  labels (§9) will tell.
- With 181 questions, one question = 0.55 points. Differences under ~2 points are within
  run-to-run noise.

**What we'd do next.** Collect 50+ real questions from classmates through the 👍/👎 log, write
the gold answers by hand, and use a judge from a different provider.

## 5. Real users: less feedback than we wanted

The app logs 👍/👎 to `data/feedback.jsonl`, but we got few real votes before the deadline, and on
Streamlit Community Cloud that file is wiped every time the app restarts. *(Team: fill in what

Next time: store feedback in a hosted database or a Google Sheet from day one.

## 6. Small things that cost us time

- We ran a quick test with `--limit 3` and it **overwrote the full results file**. Now partial
  runs never save.
- Latency was measured while two configs ran at the same time, so p95 is noisier than it would be
  in production. Both configs ran under the same conditions, so the comparison is still fair.
- PDF page numbers (used in citations) differ from the numbers printed on the pages by 1–2.
  Users looking at a printed copy may not find the cited page right away.

## 7. Growing the corpus from 2 to 14 documents

We added the 2026 catalogue, the BIM & ERP master catalogue and flyer, the BIM Day 2025 report,
the cybersecurity master brochure, the digital health master brochure and six certification
brochures (65 → 127 pages). What we learned:

- **File names lie.** `CATALOGUE_MEDTECH_180423_V8` was not a MedTech catalogue but the BIM & ERP
  master's catalogue, and `BIM_FLYER` was a report on an event. Citations show the document
  title, so we renamed the files after reading them; otherwise answers would cite the wrong thing.
- **PDF text was broken in a way you can't see.** Designer-made brochures use the ligature
  characters "ﬁ" and "ﬂ". They look normal on screen, but BM25 split "officials" into "of" +
  "cials". One line of Unicode normalisation (NFKC) fixed it.
- **Hard-coding the document list didn't scale.** The first version listed the two guides by
  name in `config.py`; a new PDF in `docs/` was silently ignored. Now every PDF is picked up and
  the index rebuilds itself when `docs/` changes.
- **The question generator made the same kinds of mistakes again.** Of 77 new questions, we
  removed 17 (`data/eval_review_log.json`): 7 duplicates (the same fact in the 2023 flyer, 2023
  catalogue and 2026 catalogue), 8 ambiguous ones ("how many modules in *the* security
  program?", when six brochures each give a different answer), 1 with a useless gold answer, and
  1 non-existent program. One more gold answer was simply wrong ("8th August" for a flyer that
  says "31st August").
- **Reading the failures found 3 more test bugs.** After the first run on the new corpus, 2 gold
  answers were incomplete (the bot was right: "no prerequisites, *but* basic networking is
  expected"; "12 hours total, 4 per week") and 1 question was impossible: the catalogue's course
  table is flattened, so courses of the three engineering programs come out interleaved. We fixed
  or removed those 3 and re-graded the same answers. We did **not** loosen the judge to accept the 4
  other strict-but-defensible failures (e.g. "starts in August" for "31st August"), because
  editing the grader after seeing the bot's score would just be moving the goalposts.
- **More documents means more ways to be confused.** Old and new documents can disagree, so the
  prompt now tells the model to prefer the most recent document and say so.

## 8. Limits we knew about and accepted

- The corpus is 127 pages, still short of "hundreds". The course syllabi would be the next addition.
- Tables (grading scale, calendar) are flattened. A table-aware parser such as `pdfplumber`
  would fix the date confusion in item 3.
- The two guides overlap, and the app searches everything (no program picker), so answers can
  mix their rules. The filter still exists in the code (`files=`) if we want it back.

## 9. Reusing our Project 1 harness

The Project 2 brief says to measure correctness "using your P1 harness". At first we wrote a new
judge and never tested it. We then reused P1: its JSON-parsing and Cohen's kappa functions are
copied unchanged into `p1_harness/`, and its judge checks run in `eval/judge_reliability.py`.

- **Position bias** (swap the order of two answers): the verdict followed the position in 1 of 30
  pairs (3.3%).
- **Verbosity bias** (pad a wrong answer with polite filler): 0 of 30 became "correct".
- **Agreement with humans: not measured yet.** `data/judge_labelling_set.jsonl` has 60 answers
  waiting for two labellers (`data/labelling_guide.md`). This is the most important P1 check, and
  until it's done the 100% faithfulness score (§4) stays unverified.

**Lesson:** a judge you have not tested is just another model with an opinion. We should have
reused the P1 checks from the first day instead of at the end.
